# Curls & Contemplation — Phase 3 LLM Council
## Question before the council
*"The forensic audit returned FIX-FIRST with 1 blocker, 13 majors, ~23 minors. The pre-mortem identified 6 tigers and 3 elephants. Is FIX-FIRST the right call, what is the optimal fix scope, and what should Michael do in the next 10 days?"*

**Method disclosure:** No subagent tooling was available; the five advisors were generated serially by a single model in independent role passes, then peer-reviewed under anonymized labels (A–E) before the Chairman's synthesis. Treat convergence with appropriate skepticism — same-model councils under-produce true disagreement.

---

## Round 1 — Advisor Positions

### Advisor A — The Contrarian
Everyone will tell you to fix everything. I'll tell you the audit is half-wrong about what matters. The fabrication findings (M6–M11) are real and lethal — agreed. But the instinct to "fix" them by adding citations is backwards. This is a trade motivational-business book for stylists, not a dissertation; readers don't want 90 more footnotes, they want a voice they trust. The braver fix is **subtraction**: cut the fake-sounding salons, cut the unverifiable quotes, and let the book stand on the anecdotes and the genuinely verified figures (Coombes, Villa, Price, Mielle). The book gets *shorter and stronger*. Second contrarian point: the team is treating the worksheet fallback (M3) as a flaw. It's actually the most honest signal in the audit — the interactive promise outgrew the EPUB format. Don't patch the CTA; ship the pack as the lead magnet it was always meant to be and make the "flaw" the funnel. Third: I'd challenge the June 18 hard stop as too generous. Every fix listed is text-level; a focused author can clear T1–T6 in four working days. The danger isn't insufficient fixing — it's that "fix-first" becomes a socially acceptable way to delay a launch that is emotionally scary. The ISBNs have been the stated blocker for weeks. Ask why they still aren't purchased. That's not a production question.

### Advisor B — First Principles
Strip it to the irreducibles. A book launch fails for exactly three reasons: (1) the product is defective, (2) the product is fine but breaches trust, (3) the product is fine and trusted but undistributed. The audit shows category 1 is nearly empty — validation clean, quizzes 16/16, math correct, one widow in 465 pages. Category 2 is where every tiger lives: fabricated examples, unlicensed scripture, bait CTAs, wrong bio. Trust breaches share a property: they are **binary and asymmetric** — invisible when fixed, catastrophic when found, and cheap to fix relative to their downside. Expected-value logic therefore says: fix every category-2 item regardless of probability, because cost-to-fix is hours and cost-of-failure is the asset itself. Category-3 work (funnels, wrap, ARC outreach) is **continuous and compounding** — every day of delay loses a day of compounding. The optimal policy falls out mechanically: do all binary trust fixes immediately and in parallel batch (they touch the same files), refuse to do continuous-quality polish (m-level) beyond what the same file-open makes free, and shift 100% of effort to distribution at the moment the last tiger dies. FIX-FIRST is correct, but only under a strict definition of "fix": trust items only. The minors are not fixes; they are improvements, and improvements after a certain point are procrastination wearing a quality costume.

### Advisor C — The Expansionist
The audit found liabilities; I see unbuilt assets. (1) The worksheet pack (M3) is a 10-touchpoint, in-book lead-capture system already typeset — gate it behind MailerLite and the *book itself* becomes the top of the Authority funnel; few first-time authors get 18 native ad placements inside their own product. (2) The audit report is content: "I forensically audited my own book before launch — here's what I found and fixed" is an extraordinary newsletter/LinkedIn series for *The Contemplation*, on-brand for a book about integrity and craft, and it pre-inoculates against exactly the attack T1 fears. Radical transparency converts the vulnerability into positioning. (3) The citation workbook plus the verified-figures list is the seed of a media/podcast pitch sheet — every verified name (Coombes, Villa, Da Costa, Christin Brown) is a potential ally, reviewer, or cross-promotion target; you've already done the research that outreach requires. (4) The NIV notice and the composite-anecdote author's note signal a level of professionalism that supports the IPPY submission narrative. (5) Longer-term: the "anonymize-or-source" sweep produces a clean factual spine that makes the audiobook, the course, and *The Artisan's Path* cheaper to produce safely. Don't just fix the book — harvest the audit. Every tiger, properly skinned, is a marketing asset.

### Advisor D — The Outsider (acquisitions editor from traditional publishing)
What I see from outside your bubble: this book is in better shape than 80% of manuscripts that cross an acquisitions desk, and the author is at the most dangerous moment of self-publishing — alone, late, and emotionally fused to the artifact. Three outsider observations. First: in trade publishing, the fabrication-pattern findings would trigger a *legal read*, not just an edit — and the legal read would flag exactly two things yours did: real-person attribution (Pecis, Nelms, the quotes) and scripture permission. Your audit independently reproduced a professional legal read. Trust it. Second: no publishing house would let "based in London" survive, because author-bio accuracy is the one thing media fact-checks 100% of the time. Third — and this is the one insiders can't see — your *interior* is over-engineered relative to your *cover and spine*, and retail is decided by the cover, the spine, the description, and the first 10% sample. The audit spent 465 pages of attention on what's behind the purchase decision and your own notes say the back+spine wrap is still the open design lever. From where I sit, the correct sequence is: trust-fixes (one batch), then **all remaining art direction hours go to the wrap and the Kindle sample experience** (front matter through Chapter I — which, notably, your audit says opens clean). Also: order the physical proof *now*; first-time KDP authors always underestimate proof-cycle latency.

### Advisor E — The Executor
Verdict accepted; here's the build plan. **Day 1–2 (Jun 10–11):** Buy/confirm ISBNs. One edit session on the copyright page: ISBNs + NIV notice + composite-anecdote author's note. Rewrite About the Author (LA). Add grep gates to the build script: `978-X`, `based in London`, `Download the Printable`. **Day 2–4 (Jun 11–13):** The T1 sweep, chapter-scoped to IV, V, XIII, XV, XVI: anonymize five Ch XIII businesses; cut/paraphrase unverifiable quotes (Erlanger details, Townsend, Kimble, Twine, King, Inuti); fix Pecis (fictionalize the mentor); confirm-or-cut Christo, Chapman-Utah, Jackie Carr; soften M11 corporate claims to "brands like." While each file is open, take the free minors in it (m8 quiz drift for that chapter, m13, m14, m15). **Day 5 (Jun 15):** Citation hygiene: fix 7 URL pairs against the bibliography, relabel XV/XVI lists, delete orphans (Tippi Shorter, Cosmo Prof), swap Woebot, fix Bandura link, hedge the Roosevelt quote. **Day 6 (Jun 16):** Worksheet pack live behind MailerLite; update fallback page to the real URL in EPUB. **Day 7 (Jun 17):** Layout batch: recto openers (M4), p434 widow, folio rule, nav/NCX 43 entries, drop printPageNumbers or add page-list. Rebuild both artifacts. **Day 8 (Jun 18):** Local epubcheck, grep gates, quiz-key spot check, order proof, upload. Hard stop. Everything not done by Jun 18 ships in a v1.1 — KDP allows content updates. Total: ~30 focused hours. The plan's only real dependency is the ISBN purchase, so do it first, today.

**C on A/B/D/E:** Convergence note — all four independently endorse cut-don't-cite, and all four treat Jun 18 as the pivot to distribution. C concedes sequencing (fix → then harvest) and amends: the transparency series launches *with* the book, framed past-tense.

## Chairman's Verdict

The council converged — unusually hard for five roles — on five points, and the convergence survives the same-model caveat because each point arrived by a different route:

1. **FIX-FIRST is affirmed, narrowly defined.** Fix means the trust-binary items only: B1, M3, M5, M6–M12, plus the composite-anecdote author's note (E1). The minor register is fixed only where a file is already open. Anything else is procrastination in a quality costume (B), or fear of launching in a production costume (A).
2. **Subtract, don't cite.** The default remedy for every unverifiable claim is deletion or anonymization, not new footnotes. The book gets shorter, safer, and more confident.
3. **June 18 is a hard stop with a v1.1 escape valve.** KDP content updates exist precisely so that m-level perfection is not a launch dependency. Day 1 actions: buy ISBNs, order proof timeline, answer the two Michael-only questions (Christo mentorship: true? Chapman/Utah: keep or cut?).
4. **The worksheet pack flips from liability to engine.** Ship it MailerLite-gated; the 18 in-book CTAs become the top of the Authority funnel.
5. **After June 18, the binding constraint is the storefront, not the interior** — back+spine wrap, book description, and the Kindle sample (which the audit confirms opens clean). All design hours reallocate there; the audit itself becomes launch content, published past-tense.

Dissent preserved for the record: A maintains the deadline should be June 16, not 18, and that the council should say plainly that the remaining blocker is a purchase, not a production task. The Chairman notes the dissent and observes that A is probably right.

— Council adjourned, June 9, 2026.
