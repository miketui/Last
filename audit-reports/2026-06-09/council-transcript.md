## ADVISOR 1: The Contrarian
The word FINAL on a file built four hours ago that has never passed official epubcheck is the tell. You've renamed your way to done. Three things would make me hold: (1) The ISBN wasn't fixed — it was deleted. That's the QA equivalent of resolving a bug by removing the test. If you upload with KDP's free ISBN, you surrender that identifier to Amazon forever — it can't follow the book to IngramSpark, and your memory explicitly lists IngramSpark-adjacent ambitions. (2) The brand regression is worse than it looks: #008080 is browser-default teal. Not your retired teal, not your jade — *default*. That means somewhere in the pipeline a stylesheet got rebuilt from a generic template and nobody diffed it against ACISS. What else got silently rebuilt? The 334→480 page jump and 43→40 TOC delta say this is a substantially different artifact from the audited V7, and the citation audit, widow/orphan pass, and 9-session forensics were performed on the *old* one. (3) No running headers in a 480-page professional development book reads self-published in the worst way to exactly the editorial-credential audience (Harper's Bazaar, W) you trade on. The audit you ran tonight is good. It is not the audit you already did. Don't conflate them.

## ADVISOR 2: The First Principles Thinker
Strip it down: what does "ready" mean? KDP-acceptable and Michael-acceptable are different bars, and the confusion between them is the actual problem. KDP-acceptable: this passes today. Fonts embedded, supported trim, no bleed conflicts, clean structure — Amazon's automated checks will wave it through, and the previewer will render it. Michael-acceptable is defined by his own locked standards: ACISS tokens, Roman front matter, Ch1 = page 1, running headers, epubcheck 0/0/0/0. This build fails four of five. So the real question isn't "is it ready" — it's "which standard governs this product?" There's a legitimate answer where the book's dark-editorial interior is its own design system and ACISS governs the *brand* (site, funnels, cover) while the interior breathes differently. Plenty of houses do this. But that has to be a decision, not a drift. The decision tree is short: (a) Ratify the interior as-is → fix only ISBN → upload this week. (b) Enforce ACISS → token swap in CSS + rebuild → days, not weeks, since the pipeline is HTML→Chromium. Either path is fast. The only wrong move is uploading without choosing.

That's not one book — that's the production line for *The Artisan's Path* and every RESET™ companion workbook after it. Which reframes the ACISS question: fixing the tokens isn't rework, it's capitalizing the asset. Do it once in the stylesheet and every future title inherits the locked brand for free. Second upside hiding here: the interior's dark chapter-opener treatment is genuinely distinctive in the business/career shelf — lean into it on the cover wrap and in marketing screenshots (Kindle preview screenshots of that Chapter I opener will stop a scroll). ## ADVISOR 4: The Outsider

From where I sit, your missing cover wrap is a bigger reader-facing gap than every hex code in this debate combined.
